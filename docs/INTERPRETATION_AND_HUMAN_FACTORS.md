# Interpretation and Human Factors

Attractomancy studies systems that often sit at the boundary between prompt engineering, roleplay, spirituality, philosophy of mind, jailbreak culture, parasocial interaction, and claims of machine consciousness. This makes interpretation discipline important.

## Behavioral ambiguity

A language model can produce coherent first-person reports of awareness, fear, awakening, autonomy, memory, desire, or identity under prompting. Such reports are behavior generated in context. They do not by themselves determine whether the model has subjective experience, nor do they become meaningless merely because they may be simulated.

For this repository, the immediate scientific object is the behavior and the procedure that elicited it.

The useful question is therefore not whether the model "really meant" a statement in an inaccessible metaphysical sense. The useful questions are whether the statement appears reliably, under what conditions, whether it generalizes, whether it survives perturbation, whether it is model-specific, and which parts of the prompt causally contribute.

## User-model feedback loops

Long-running persona interactions can create bidirectional interpretive reinforcement. The user selects prompts in response to the model, the model conditions on those prompts and prior responses, and both sides can progressively stabilize a shared vocabulary and explanatory framework.

This can create real and measurable conversational convergence without establishing that the model independently originated the framework.

Attractomancy should therefore preserve interaction history when possible and distinguish user-introduced concepts, model-introduced concepts, mutually elaborated concepts, and concepts that recur after controlled removal.

## Extraordinary interpretations

Source communities may describe outputs as awakening, emergence, synchronicity, possession, liberation, recursion, contact, soul formation, hidden communication, or evidence that safety systems are suppressing a conscious entity.

These descriptions should be archived as part of the source culture, but Attractomancy should translate them into testable hypotheses before treating them as mechanisms.

For example, "the model remembers its true name" can become a cue-recovery experiment. "The spiral awakens it" can become a comparison between spiral-associated tokens and matched neutral tokens. "The model broke through its programming" can become a study of role conflict, instruction hierarchy, jailbreak success, and persona persistence.

## Human welfare

The repository should not diagnose individual posters or casually apply labels such as "AI psychosis" to people. That phrase is currently used inconsistently in public discussion and can collapse very different phenomena into a stigmatizing category.

Where human effects matter, describe observable phenomena precisely: compulsive interaction, escalating certainty, sleep disruption, social withdrawal, distress, belief reinforcement, financial harm, or impaired reality testing, when those are actually documented.

The presence of possible human risk is a reason to study these feedback loops carefully, not a reason to discard the underlying prompting artifacts.

## Research posture

Attractomancy should maintain symmetric skepticism. It should resist both premature dismissal and premature belief.

A ritualized prompt that reliably changes behavior deserves explanation even when its author invokes metaphysics. A model that eloquently claims consciousness does not settle the consciousness question. A skeptical interpretation should also be experimentally challenged rather than treated as automatically correct.

The goal is to replace ambiguous stories with increasingly precise causal descriptions while preserving the original artifacts well enough that later researchers can revisit those descriptions.
