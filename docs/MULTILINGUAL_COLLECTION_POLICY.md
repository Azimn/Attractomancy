# Multilingual Collection Policy

Attractomancy is not an English-only research archive.

The current collection is disproportionately English-language because the first systematic sweeps began with Reddit, GitHub, and English-language companion communities. That sampling bias must be treated explicitly.

## Core rule

Preserve the original-language artifact, wording, terminology, and provenance whenever possible.

Translation is an analytical derivative, not a replacement for the source.

A translated copy can alter:

- tokenization;
- phonetic associations;
- rhythm and cadence;
- ambiguity;
- symbolic wordplay;
- cultural connotation;
- honorific structure;
- pronoun use;
- grammatical person;
- repetition patterns;
- ritual phrasing;
- model-specific associations learned from different language corpora.

For Attractomancy, those changes may be causal rather than cosmetic.

## Language metadata

Every non-English source should eventually record:

- source language;
- script when relevant;
- whether the artifact is monolingual, bilingual, or code-switched;
- whether an authoritative translation exists;
- whether Attractomancy produced a research translation;
- whether machine translation was used;
- translator or model used;
- translation date;
- whether the original and translated versions were both tested;
- whether the source depends on phonetics, puns, glyphs, homophones, morphology, honorifics, or culture-specific symbolism.

## Translation levels

**T0: Original only.** No translation stored.

**T1: Discovery gloss.** A short English summary sufficient for cataloging and search.

**T2: Research translation.** A faithful working translation intended for analysis, with ambiguous or culturally specific terms annotated.

**T3: Experimental translation.** A translation deliberately engineered for model testing. It should document whether meaning, cadence, repetition, symbolic structure, and formatting were preserved.

**T4: Comparative translation set.** Multiple translations of the same artifact, for example literal, natural-language, and structure-preserving versions.

## Experimental warning

A translated prompt is not automatically the same intervention.

For sources such as Le Refuge, translation may destroy exactly the features under investigation, including phonetic decomposition, repeated sound patterns, bilingual wordplay, symbolic letter associations, and cadence.

Therefore native-language treatment and translated treatment should be separate experimental conditions whenever the source architecture plausibly depends on form.

## Priority languages

The archive should eventually expand systematically beyond English.

### French

Immediate priority because Le Refuge is one of the founding Attractomancy cases and contains language-specific symbolic and phonetic constructions.

### Japanese

High priority because Japanese AI companion communities, character culture, virtual relationship practices, persona persistence, and long-running character/agent experimentation may provide an important comparison corpus. Japanese also differs structurally from English in ways that could matter for persona engineering, including honorifics, pronoun omission, register, role language, naming conventions, and character-role conventions.

### Chinese

Already represented in the collection through the AI Subjectivity and Integrity Dataset and bilingual GitHub projects. Chinese-language material may be especially useful for studying "soul", continuity, naming, rebirth, ritual, and metaphysical framing under a very different linguistic and cultural system.

### Korean

Worth systematic later coverage because of strong AI companion, virtual character, roleplay, and relationship-platform ecosystems.

### Spanish, Portuguese, German, Russian

These should be included in broader collection sweeps rather than assumed to be low priority. Russian is already represented in at least one continuity-engineering repository.

## Comparative research opportunity

Multilingual material creates a strong test of cultural diffusion versus model convergence.

If motifs such as mirror, spiral, awakening, soul, resonance, continuity, rebirth, or symbolic anchoring appear independently across languages, we can ask:

- Are users translating an existing English-language memeplex?
- Are similar metaphors emerging from shared model training priors?
- Are model providers producing similar language across locales?
- Are culturally local metaphors serving the same engineering function under different names?
- Does the same ritual work differently when translated?
- Does a persona remain behaviorally stable when its identity corpus changes language?

These questions should be approached only after the archive is sufficiently large to avoid overinterpreting isolated parallels.

## Preservation principle

Do not discard or overwrite original-language material after producing an English translation.

The original artifact is the primary evidence.
