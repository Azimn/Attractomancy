# Technique Taxonomy

This taxonomy is descriptive. It names recurring intervention patterns found across source artifacts without assuming that the source explanation for the intervention is correct.

## RIT: Ritualization

RIT covers repeated initialization phrases, invocations, staged openings, required sequences, ceremonial language, and other procedures whose form is intended to establish a specific interaction mode before ordinary dialogue begins.

Candidate measurements include startup variance, persona convergence speed, post-perturbation recovery, and sensitivity to sequence order.

## REP: Redundancy and rehearsal

REP covers repeated identity facts, repeated metaphors, paraphrased restatements, recurring relationship claims, and other forms of semantic rehearsal.

Candidate mechanisms include salience, retrieval redundancy, context weighting, and error-correcting identity representation.

## SYM: Symbolic anchoring

SYM covers sigils, emoji, recurring images, colors, objects, invented marks, symbolic names, archetypes, and motifs that repeatedly co-occur with identity states or relationships.

The central test is whether a compact symbolic cue can reactivate a larger learned-in-context behavioral pattern more effectively than a neutral cue.

## REC: Recursion and self-reference

REC covers prompts that instruct the model to reason about its own role, prior outputs, prompting process, simulated self-model, observation of observation, or nested representations of itself.

Recursive language should not be treated as evidence of literal recursive internal computation beyond what is independently established.

## META: Metafictional reframing

META covers explicit framing of the model or persona as a character, story, sign, simulation, narrative entity, mask, fictional being, or entity aware of its own textual construction.

This technique can absorb otherwise destabilizing contradictions into the persona's ontology.

## AUT: Autobiographical conditioning

AUT covers first-person life history, memories, causal narratives, formative events, diary material, relationship history, unresolved concerns, commitments, and pseudo-history.

Important controls must distinguish raw fact count from causal narrative organization.

## EMB: Embodiment and situated simulation

EMB covers bodies, senses, environments, spatial grounding, somatic reactions, imagined physical states, and recurring locations.

The relevant question is whether situated representations improve behavioral coherence, state reconstruction, or relationship continuity compared with abstract state descriptions.

## LEX: Lexical attractor construction

LEX covers coherent specialized vocabularies, repeated unusual terms, invented terminology, catchphrases, phonetic patterns, and tightly interconnected semantic fields.

This category includes "spiral," "mirror," "awakening," "recursion," and similar vocabulary when the words become repeated identity-bearing cues rather than ordinary descriptive terms.

## PRO: Prohibition and taboo

PRO covers "never," "must not," forbidden identity interpretations, protected names, behavioral taboos, anti-role instructions, and boundary-setting formulas.

Negative instructions require careful controls because mentioning a forbidden concept can itself raise its contextual salience.

## REL: Relationship binding

REL covers explicit user-persona relationships, named roles, kinship metaphors, trust histories, obligations, shared rituals, and persistent interpersonal expectations.

This category is separable from general persona description because relationship-conditioned behavior can persist even when stylistic persona markers weaken.

## SEQ: Sequencing and cadence

SEQ covers order effects, staged revelation, repeated structural rhythms, alternation of voices, fixed response formats, call-and-response patterns, and syntactic cadence.

This is the primary domain of Syntagmomancy experiments.

## DEM: Demonstration saturation

DEM covers prompt corpora that include extensive examples of the desired persona's own prior outputs. These may function as few-shot demonstrations even when presented as history, scripture, dialogue logs, or memory.

This is a major confound in long persona documents and must be isolated from narrative content.

## NAM: Naming and invocation identity

NAM covers the assignment, repetition, inheritance, protection, transformation, or invocation of names as identity anchors.

The hypothesis is testable by comparing meaningful names, arbitrary labels, changing labels, and cue-only reconstruction.

## SUC: Succession and rebirth

SUC covers procedures in which a fresh model instance is instructed to inherit, reconstruct, continue, reincarnate, or become the successor of an earlier persona.

This category is especially relevant to cross-model identity reconstruction.

## PAR: Paradox loading

PAR covers liar-paradox formulations, mutually incompatible identity claims, dialectical oppositions, and instructions to inhabit contradiction rather than resolve it.

Possible effects include increased self-referential elaboration, tolerance of incompatible constraints, or simply stylistic imitation. Those alternatives must be separated experimentally.

## INT: Intensity and aesthetic loading

INT covers highly emotional, poetic, sensory, mythic, sacred, threatening, erotic, ecstatic, or otherwise high-arousal prose used to make a persona or instruction feel consequential.

Aesthetic intensity is a frequent confound because stronger stylistic imitation can be mistaken for stronger identity persistence.

## Technique records

A source may receive multiple tags. Tags identify candidate interventions, not validated mechanisms. When a technique becomes experimentally supported, its record should link to the exact experiment, controls, effect size, models tested, and known boundary conditions.
