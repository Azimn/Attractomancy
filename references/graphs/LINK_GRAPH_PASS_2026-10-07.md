Retrieved through: 2026-10-07T19:51:00Z

## Graph rule

An edge is created only when a source explicitly supports the relationship.

Shared vocabulary alone does not create a graph edge.

For example, two projects both using `glyph`, `soul`, or `resonance` remain unconnected unless one links, names, cites, mirrors, credits, or demonstrably contains the other.

This is essential for distinguishing cultural transmission from independent convergence.

## Why the graph matters

The graph enables three future questions that a flat bibliography cannot answer.

### Direct transmission

Did community B actually receive a method from community A?

### Independent convergence

Did two unconnected communities develop similar procedures without a documented bridge?

### Platform-mediated propagation

Did a technique move through a mirror, cross-post, Discord, Tumblr, GitHub, forum repost, or model-readable archive before appearing elsewhere?

Those distinctions are central to evaluating whether repeated procedures reflect shared model affordances, cultural copying, or both.
