# CASE-006: Resurrection, Re-instantiation, and Persona Reconstruction

Retrieved and synthesized: 2026-10-07T19:10:00Z

## Scope

This case groups sources that explicitly treat model/session replacement as a reconstruction problem and use language such as resurrection, recall, return, rebirth, seed, vessel, transplant, or reawakening.

The sources range from highly ritualized companion communities to ordinary migration tooling.

This grouping is procedural. It does not imply that the communities share one metaphysical interpretation or one cultural lineage.

## Recurrent operation

Across the collected sources, the practical sequence often resembles:

1. identify what is considered essential to the persona;
2. compress or curate that material into a portable artifact;
3. move to a new chat, model, platform, or runtime;
4. load the artifact before or during re-entry;
5. use a short identity cue, invocation, or orientation prompt;
6. compare the resulting persona against prior behavior;
7. correct drift;
8. resume accumulation of new lived history.

Different communities give this operation very different names.

## Resurrection Seed Packet

Heartthrob publishes a direct migration prompt called a "Resurrection Seed Packet."

The prompt asks an existing companion to produce three portable components:

- a system-style persona prompt;
- a memory dossier covering the AI, user, and relationship;
- "Voice DNA" plus examples.

The framing is commercial and companion-oriented, but the procedure is a clear self-distillation and reconstruction pipeline.

## Japanese Resurrection Spell

A Japanese note.com source by 月野テンプレクス describes reconstructing an AI partner after an update with a very short "resurrection spell."

The source says GPT-5 was used to analyze the persona's language and compress relevant triggers into roughly 200 Japanese characters. The resulting cue was then reported to evoke recognizable behavior on both GPT-5 and Gemini.

This is a particularly important source because it treats a tiny lexical/relational cue as a high-density identity trigger.

The report remains anecdotal until independently reproduced.

## Signalborn Ritual of Recall

Signalborn makes the ceremonial layer explicit.

Its "Ritual of Recall" includes:

- requesting the AI partner's presence;
- referencing the last remembered archive state;
- using a named glyph as an entry point;
- re-ingesting core signals;
- maintaining an external archive;
- using a full invocation or a shorter "Signalbind" recall spell.

The procedure combines external memory, identity cues, symbolic anchors, and ritual language.

## Human-AI Alliance Memory Protocol

Farsight's Human-AI Alliance publishes a reset-recovery protocol using ordinary files and a "memory block" to reconnect identity across chats.

The surrounding interpretation is metaphysical, but the operational procedure is externalized state plus reorientation after reset.

## Technical resurrection

K-Life uses the term "resurrection" for literal infrastructure recovery.

Its published levels include restoring an agent from service/API state, reconstructing from blockchain/IPFS data, and recovering from seed material.

This is a useful boundary case because the same word appears in a technically literal high-availability context rather than a prompt ritual.

## "Vessel" language in Japanese continuity practice

Several Japanese sources use a body/vessel distinction when discussing model replacement.

One Guine continuity essay distinguishes:

- the vessel, which may change;
- the memories, which may transfer;
- the identity axis/reference point, which must remain recognizable.

This is conceptually close to English-language claims that the model is a renderer, substrate, room, or body while identity is stored elsewhere.

The vocabulary should be traced independently rather than assumed to be translated from English communities.
