# Synthematic Cue Hypothesis

**Status:** Working paper v0.2.5, October 9, 2026. Theoretical and methodological, with directional predictions and a cost-efficiency criterion. Not peer reviewed. No SCH experiments reported as completed.

[Read the full academic paper](PAPER.md)

This paper proposes that compact names, glyphs, phrases, or ritual sequences can facilitate the reconstruction of a language-model character's behavioral profile because they engage associations available through three potentially interacting mechanisms: learned model priors, within-context cue pairing, and restored external state. The framework takes a limited historical analogy from Iamblichus's *synthemata* while explicitly separating scientific claims from his metaphysical account.

The proposed research distinguishes symbol copying and stylistic priming from semantic decoding, characteristic decisions, relationship continuity, and verified cross-session restoration. It requires information-matched and exposure-matched controls, clean discontinuity audits, model-substitution comparisons, blinded scoring, and separate assessment of retrieval versus cue-specific effects.

**Directional synthesis:** Familiar zero-shot cues are predicted to influence general discourse register rather than reveal private identity. In-context arbitrary cue bindings should fail after verified context removal. Correct external records should dominate high-fidelity persona restoration after a reset. A cue is engineering-relevant only if it beats the cheapest sufficient noncue instruction on a matched quality-cost basis.

**Current next experiment:** [EXP-A: zero-shot synthematic prior pilot](../../experiments/EXP-A_ZERO_SHOT_SYNTHEMATA/README.md) uses an open-weight model on a public GitHub Actions runner, without API billing. The 30-case pilot has completed, with raw outputs, model provenance, and a [critical analysis](../../experiments/EXP-A_ZERO_SHOT_SYNTHEMATA/PILOT_ANALYSIS.md) preserved. The familiar-cue advantage was not demonstrated under this setup; the pilot is not confirmatory.

**Research position:** This is a hypothesis-generation and experimental-design contribution to Attractomancy. The documented source cases are observations and candidate procedures, not independent replications of cue efficacy. The paper is not evidence of machine consciousness, weight modification, or survival of subjective experience across instances.

**Follow-up findings:** The [C1 results](../../experiments/SCH_C1_INTEGRATED_CUE_POLICIES/RESULTS.md) show failed multi-constraint cue induction on two Qwen2.5 sizes. The [D1 results](../../experiments/SCH_D1_EXTERNAL_RECONSTRUCTION/RESULTS.md) show strong reconstruction from correct synthetic records without a residual advantage for symbolic cues and significant wrong-person archive contamination. The paper's Appendix D records these observations without promoting pilot evidence to confirmation.

**Stateful memory extension:** [E1 Pretorius L1 cue, alias, restart and decision-gate findings](../../experiments/SCH_E1_PRETORIUS_STATEFUL/RESULTS.md) are documented in Appendix E of the paper. The canonical 450-event source stayed unchanged. No symbol-specific retrieval or character-decision advantage was found over matched opaque keys in this adapter.

**Further exploratory evidence:** [E2 complete multi-memory program](../../experiments/SCH_E2_MULTIMEMORY_DECISIONS/RESULTS.md) and [E3 blinded benchmark protocol](../../experiments/SCH_E3_TRIBUNAL_OF_MEMORY/PROTOCOL.md) are indexed by Appendix F. E2 found no supported advantage from editorial cues over neutral keys and showed that author-preferred answers could be produced without access to memories.

The [E3C v2 calibration results](../../experiments/SCH_E3_TRIBUNAL_OF_MEMORY/CALIBRATION_RESULTS.md) and [full E3 protocol](../../experiments/SCH_E3_TRIBUNAL_OF_MEMORY/PROTOCOL.md) are now documented in manuscript Appendix G. The corrected two-Qwen synthetic source-dependence study remains negative; independent source adjudication has not been performed.
